01 / 16

The game in 60 seconds

Chess-like tactics with monsters you raise

Gameplay videoLoading the 96-second gameplay capture…
  • Board: a 12×12 isometric grid with high ground (+30% damage, +1 range), water, 1-wide bridges and walls that block line of sight.
  • Turn: initiative order. Each monster moves, then picks Attack, Skill, Defend or Wait.
  • Summon: your Sigil calls up to 2 squad monsters a turn onto spawn tiles and the Prism (capsule) nodes you've taken.
  • Win: destroy the rival's Symbol, or hold 3 relic nodes where the stage says so. You lose if your Sigil falls.
  • Grow: monsters level up, evolve at Lv16 and Lv36, and fuse in pairs into new monsters.

Source: README.md, HANDOFF.md (2026-10-21).

Strategy and RPG: two of the largest mobile genres

2024: Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports[5]. 2025 strategy: Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports[4].

How to read this

Most of the big numbers come from gacha (paid random draws) run by large studios. Aether Sigil sells no paid random draws, so treat those numbers as the size of the audience, not as a benchmark for us.

The closest model

Balatro shows a premium game with no ads and no IAP can earn millions on mobile. It arrived there after a big PC hit, which we don't have.

Indie scale

Indie monster collectors on PC reach roughly 100k–500k copies (Coromon, Temtem). That's a realistic order of magnitude for a strong indie launch.

Estimates from trackers (Sensor Tower, AppMagic) cover store spending only, and the firms measure it differently. Other titles we looked at (Into the Breach, Cassette Beasts, Monster Sanctuary, Super Auto Pets) have no verified figures, so they're left out.

Monetization

Fair by design: no pay-to-win, no loot boxes

OptionProsConsRegulation and policy
Premium unlock (first tower free)One fair price. No pay-to-win. Fits a 120-stage campaign. The free tower acts as the demo.Mobile players resist paying up front, and income stops once the campaign is sold.Low risk. Name the price clearly on the store page.
Cosmetics (skins, board themes, Sigil styles)Doesn't touch balance. Fits anime collectors. Keeps earning after launch.Needs extra art for every item. Small at first.Sell named, fixed items only. Never a random draw.

Ad-supported free tier (rewarded ads)Earns from players who never pay. Rewarded ads are opt-in.Ad revenue per user is low, and ads cheapen a premium anime look. eCPM is unverified, so ads are left out of the model.Needs privacy and consent work (data-safety forms, ATT).

Recommended launch mix

Tower 1 free, then one unlock for towers 2–12, plus cosmetic-only IAP (roadmap issue #40). A season pass comes later, once there's data. Capsules stay earn-only: selling random capsule contents would make them a loot box.

Order

Durations

Only the M5 figure is sourced (Google's 12 testers × 14 days, about 3 weeks).[48] Everything marked * is an estimate. Added up, that's launch in about 4–8 months from the start of M4 if M4–M7 run in sequence (19–36 weeks), or roughly 6–12 months from today including the M1–M3 close-out.

Formula

Gross = downloads × unlock rate × $4.99 + downloads × cosmetic-buyer rate × average spend. Net = gross × (1 − store fee).

Net ignores VAT/GST (the stores deduct it), refunds and income tax.

Where the inputs come from

  • Downloads, unlock rate, price and cosmetic spend are all assumptions. There is no paid user acquisition in any scenario.
  • Anchor: only 1.83% of mobile players make any in-app purchase (Unity 2024).[30] A paid unlock after a free tower could convert higher than that, but nothing we found proves it.
  • Retention context: the median game keeps about 22% of players to day 1 and just under 4% to day 7 (GameAnalytics 2026).[29]

Against year-one costs (lean $624–$2,124; outsourced $17,634–$60,828): Conservative ($976 net) covers only the low end of the lean path; Base ($10,183 net) covers the whole lean path, but not outsourced polish; Optimistic ($84,872 net) covers even the high end of outsourced polish.

11 / 16

Benefits of publishing

Why ship it rather than keep polishing

Revenue potential

Illustrative year-one net of $976 / $10,183 / $84,872. Small at the low end, but every extra download earns at near-zero marginal cost. Balatro shows the upside for a premium game on mobile.[15]

An owned IP and brand

1,000 original monsters with 0 exact name collisions against Pokémon, Yu-Gi-Oh and Digimon, 12 towers, 12 champions and 486 dialogue lines, all held in one data bible. A live game makes the brand real enough to extend: Steam PC (Steam Direct costs $100[33]), a sequel, an art book, plushies and merch.

Portfolio and credibility

A shipped game on two stores, with public ratings and download counts, is evidence a publisher or investor can check. A prototype isn't. Publishing deals with an advance averaged a 55/45 split in the developer's favour in GameDiscoverCo's sample, and traction is what gets you those terms.[40]

Community and data

Real funnels (tutorial, stage drop-off) replace AI-vs-AI guesses. The balance curve is currently tuned on simulations only. Discord and devlogs build a group that wishlists the PC version and brings launch-day reviews.[45]

The AI-agent pipeline as an asset

One founder plus agents produced 18,623 green checks, a reproducible balance script, an idempotent decisions script and 41 issues with acceptance criteria. Shipping proves the method works, and the method carries over to the next title. (The studio framework is third-party MIT, so the asset is the process, not the tool.)

Low capital at risk

$124 in cash reaches both stores. The lean path is about $624–$2,124. Spend can grow only after the data says yes: soft launch first, art budget second.

13 / 16

Go-to-market

Test small, learn, then widen

1 · Closed test

Google's 12 testers × 14 days (M5)[48], recruited from Discord. Measure the tutorial and Tower 1 funnel.

2 · Soft launch

An English-speaking test market. Historically that meant Canada (72% of games studied), Australia (56%) and the Philippines (44%) (2016 data).[38] The Philippines is now used in about half of soft launches, with installs under $0.50.[39]

3 · Global launch

Go wide only if day-1 and day-7 retention reach the median benchmarks (~22% / ~4%).[29]

Community

Discord for testers and launch-day reviews.[45] TikTok devlogs: one 571k-view video brought about 2,900 Steam wishlists in a case study.[44]

Press kit

Human-made key art, a 30–60 s gameplay trailer, a fact sheet (120 stages, 1,000 monsters, no loot boxes) and a founder story about the AI agent team.

Steam PC, later

Steam Direct costs $100.[33] Next Fest runs 3× a year with a demo, and a game gets one fest.[47] Median wishlist-to-sales conversion is now about 0.15× (0.10× above $10).[46]

Publisher vs self-publish

Self-publish first, and keep the publisher door open

DimensionSelf-publishWith a publisher
Revenue share100% after store feesNo-advance deals averaged ~71% to the developer. With a $100k–500k advance, ~55/45 to the developer. Some recoup the advance +15% first, then split 50/50.[40]
Cash up frontNone: self-funded ($124 minimum)An advance can pay for the art pass
Marketing and user acquisitionFounder's time and small testsThe publisher's channels, press contacts and UA budget
Control and IPFull control, IP keptDepends on the contract. Read the IP, term and recoup clauses.
SpeedAs fast as Shady and the agents can goContracting takes time, plus the publisher's milestone gates
Platform programmesApple Arcade takes pitches (no ads, no IAP).[41] Google Play Pass is invitation-only, with royalties set by Google's own algorithm.[42]Arcade payouts are reported to be falling (anonymous sources).[43]

Recommendation

Self-publish the mobile soft launch. Capital needed is low, and the data it produces is exactly what a publisher would ask for. If retention clears the median benchmarks, pitch publishers for the art pass, the global launch and the Steam port, from a position of evidence.

The publisher split data comes from a small 2020 sample (Voyer Law, via GameDiscoverCo). Use it as a starting range for negotiation, not a quote.
